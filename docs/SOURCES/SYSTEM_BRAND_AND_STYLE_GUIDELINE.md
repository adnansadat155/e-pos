## Section 1 — Brand Strategy & Foundation

### 1.1 Brand Purpose

Dinevique exists to restore clarity to restaurant operations. Behind every great dining experience is a team managing dozens of moving parts — orders, tickets, tables, kitchens, and cash — often with technology that fragments rather than unifies their work. Dinevique brings those parts into a single, coherent, connected platform so operators can focus on hospitality rather than troubleshooting their tools.

The brand was founded on a conviction: that restaurant technology should be as resilient as the people who use it — capable of functioning through outages, evolving with the business, and disappearing seamlessly into the rhythm of a well-run service.

### 1.2 Brand Positioning Statement

> For independent restaurant operators, fast-casual groups, and full-service dining businesses who need their technology to keep pace with their service, **Dinevique** is the hospitality operations platform that unifies point of sale, kitchen coordination, handheld ordering, and cloud management into one connected, resilient experience. Unlike fragmented POS vendors that require multiple tools and fail under network pressure, Dinevique is engineered for continuity — keeping kitchens printing, tables moving, and teams coordinated even when the internet drops.

### 1.3 Brand Essence

> **Connected clarity for every service.**

*(Derived from tagline + vision + platform architecture)*

### 1.4 Vision & Mission

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Vision** | To help restaurants operate with greater clarity, resilience, and consistency through accessible, connected technology. |
| **Mission** | To simplify restaurant workflows by connecting orders, service teams, kitchen operations, and management tools in a practical platform built for everyday service. |

### 1.5 Core Values

| Value | Definition | Behavioral Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Connected by Design** | Every component of the platform is built to work as a unified system, not a collection of tools. | We never ship a feature that creates a workflow break. Integration is non-negotiable. |
| **Resilient by Default** | The platform is designed to function under real-world conditions — intermittent connectivity, hardware variation, and peak service loads. | Offline continuity is a core engineering requirement, not a premium add-on. |
| **Clarity for Teams** | Complexity is absorbed by the platform, not pushed onto the people using it. | Every interface decision prioritizes operational legibility over feature density. |
| **Practical Growth** | We build foundations, not ceilings — features should scale with the business, not constrain it. | Roadmap prioritization favors extensibility: multi-terminal, multi-location, multi-channel. |
| **Trusted Hospitality** | Restaurants trust Dinevique with their revenue, their orders, and their teams. That trust is treated as sacred. | Reliability, security, and honest product communication come before marketing claims. |

### 1.6 Brand Personality Spectrum

| Axis | Position | Rationale |

| **Formal vs. Casual** | 60% Formal | Professional-grade tools for operators; approachable language without being flippant. |
| **Premium vs. Budget** | 65% Premium | Positioned above budget entry tools; not inaccessible luxury — earned premium through capability. |
| **Serious vs. Playful** | 70% Serious | Restaurant operations are high-stakes; the platform tone reflects this. Warmth exists; levity does not. |
| **Corporate vs. Human** | 55% Human | Built for real teams doing real work; not enterprise-distant, not casual-startup either. |

| **Authoritative vs. Supportive** | 58% Authoritative | The platform sets the standard for how restaurant operations should run; but it supports, not lectures, operators. |

### 1.7 Brand Archetypes

**Primary Archetype: The Ruler**
Dinevique creates order in environments defined by chaos. It establishes systems, coordinates teams, and ensures that every part of the operation operates within a coherent, managed structure. The Ruler archetype is appropriate because the platform literally governs the flow of information through a restaurant — from the first order entry to kitchen ticket completion to cloud synchronization.

**Secondary Archetype: The Sage**
The platform embeds deep operational intelligence — offline queuing logic, idempotent cloud ingestion, multi-terminal WebSocket coordination — and surfaces that intelligence through clarity, not complexity. The Sage gives teams confidence: the system knows what it's doing, even when the internet does not.

## Section 2 — Brand Architecture

### 2.1 Brand Hierarchy

```
DINEVIQUE (Master Brand)
│
├── Dinevique POS            (Platform Module — Front of House)
├── Dinevique Kitchen        (Platform Module — Kitchen Display Board)
├── Dinevique Handheld       (Platform Module — Mobile Waitstaff)
├── Dinevique Online         (Platform Module — Web Storefront)
└── Dinevique Cloud          (Platform Module — Admin & Management Portal)
```

Dinevique operates as a **single-brand, modular architecture**. All product modules draw identity from the master brand. No module operates as an independent sub-brand. *(Derived from platform architecture documentation)*

### 2.2 Brand Relationship Rules

| Context | Rule |
|---|---|
| Module naming | Always prefix with "Dinevique" — never standalone (e.g., "Dinevique Kitchen", not just "Kitchen Display") |
| Master vs. module | Master brand appears first and with greater visual weight in all co-presentations |
| Partner integrations | Dinevique name leads; partner name follows. Format: "Dinevique × [Partner]" |
| Legal / formal contexts | Use full legal name as confirmed during entity registration |
| Client-facing materials | Use "Dinevique" exclusively — no acronyms (e.g., not "DV" or "DNQ") |

### 2.3 Naming Convention

| Element | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Master Brand | Title case, single word | Dinevique |
| Platform Module | Dinevique + Noun | Dinevique Kitchen |
| Feature Reference | lowercase in body text | the kitchen display, the handheld ordering flow |
| File Names | kebab-case | dinevique-kitchen-spec.pdf |
| Internal Event Codes | SCREAMING_SNAKE | `TICKET_RECEIVED`, `ORDER_CREATE` |

### 2.4 Tagline Usage

| Context | Format |
|---|---|
| Hero / campaign materials | *The Art of Smarter Dining.* (italics, full stop) |
| Email signatures | The Art of Smarter Dining. (no italics, full stop) |

| Internal documents | Optional — use at discretion |

## Section 3 — Logo System & Usage

### 3.1 Logo Philosophy

The Dinevique logo mark is conceived as an expression of orchestrated connectivity — form that communicates coordination without complexity. The wordmark stands as the primary brand identifier in all professional contexts, typeset with precision to reflect the platform's polished, operational character. The icon mark, when finalized, should embody the idea of a connected node: a system that touches multiple points simultaneously while maintaining singular coherence. Every variant of the logo is designed to communicate confidence without ostentation — fitting for a brand trusted by operators across service types.

*[TBD — Final logo assets pending design finalization and legal/domain clearance for the Dinevique name]*

### 3.2 Logo Variants

| Variant | Components | Primary Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Lockup** | Icon Mark + Wordmark + Tagline | Hero placements, cover pages, brand launch materials |
| **Standard Lockup** | Icon Mark + Wordmark | Website header, app launch screens, presentations |
| **Wordmark Only** | Wordmark | Email signatures, documents, narrow horizontal spaces |
| **Icon Mark Only** | Icon Mark | App icon, social media avatar, favicon, small placements |
| **Reversed (White)** | Full or Standard on dark background | Dark mode, dark print backgrounds, video end frames |
| **Monochrome (Black)** | Full or Standard in single black | Single-color print, embossing, legal documents |

### 3.3 Logo Components

**Icon Mark:** To be designed around the concept of networked coordination — representing the Master Terminal as the central orchestrator with connected modules. The mark should be geometric, clean, and scalable to 16x16px without loss of legibility.

**Wordmark:** "DINEVIQUE" set in the primary brand typeface at designated tracking. The word construction blends "dine" (hospitality) and "unique" (differentiation) — a subtlety the wordmark should honor through refined, unhurried letterforms.

**Tagline Treatment:** *The Art of Smarter Dining.* set in the secondary typeface at 40% of the wordmark's x-height. Always in the brand's lighter weight. Never bolder than the wordmark.

### 3.4 Logo Clear Space

Minimum clear space around the logo equals the height of the icon mark (designated as **1x**). No text, imagery, or graphic elements may intrude upon this protected zone.

```
         +----------------------------------------------+
  1x up  |                                              |
         |   [ICON]  DINEVIQUE                          |
  1x dn  |         The Art of Smarter Dining.           |
         +----------------------------------------------+
         <- 1x ->                              <- 1x ->
```

### 3.5 Logo Placement Rules

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Minimum width — Standard Lockup** | 120px digital / 32mm print |
| **Minimum width — Icon Mark only** | 24px digital / 8mm print |
| **Preferred backgrounds** | Brand Onyx (#0D0D0D), Brand Ivory (#F7F5F1), Brand Slate (#1E2A38) |
| **Alignment** | Left-aligned in documents; centered on cover pages and social media |
| **Orientation** | Horizontal lockup is primary; vertical stacking only with explicit approval |
| **Color on backgrounds** | Dark background: White reversed; Light background: Brand Onyx or Brand Ink |

### 3.6 Logo Misuse

The following uses are strictly prohibited:

1. Stretching or compressing the logo in any axis
2. Applying drop shadows, glows, bevels, or emboss effects
3. Placing the logo on a photographic background without sufficient contrast
4. Rotating the logo at any angle
5. Recreating the logo in an alternative typeface
6. Using the tagline without the wordmark
7. Adding outline strokes to the wordmark or icon mark
8. Placing the logo on a patterned or gradient background that reduces legibility
9. Using an unapproved color version
10. Cropping or obscuring any element of the logo lockup

### 3.7 Co-branding Rules

| Context | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hardware partners (e.g., Epson, Sunmi) | Dinevique logo leads; partner logo at equal size, separated by one clear space unit + divider line |
| Technology integrations | Identify in text only ("Integrated with [Partner]") — no logo lock-up unless formal agreement exists |
| Event / conference signage | Dinevique logo at minimum 60% of total logo cluster size |

## Section 4 — Color System

### 4.1 Strategic Color Intent

The Dinevique palette is built around the duality of hospitality: the warmth of a well-lit dining room and the precision of the back-of-house. Deep, authoritative darks establish operational trust; warm neutrals create approachability; and a restrained amber accent communicates energy and action without aggression. The palette is designed to function at WCAG AA compliance for all primary text-on-background combinations, ensuring accessibility across diverse display environments — from kitchen tablets in bright ambient light to dashboard screens in dim back-of-house offices.

## Section 5 — Typography System

### 5.1 Type Philosophy

Dinevique's typographic system prioritizes operational legibility and editorial refinement in equal measure. The primary typeface is selected for its performance under the demanding conditions of restaurant technology: screen glare, fast scanning, and small handheld displays. The secondary typeface provides warmth and hierarchy distinction for editorial and marketing contexts. Together, they communicate the brand's dual identity — a tool precise enough for professionals, approachable enough for any operator.

*(Note: Typefaces below are recommended based on brand positioning and must be licensed appropriately for all deployment contexts — including web, native app, and print.)*

### 5.2 Primary Typeface — Inter

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Family** | Inter |
| **Classification** | Humanist Sans-Serif |
| **License** | Open Font License (Google Fonts) |
| **Primary Roles** | UI text, navigation, body copy, data tables, labels, all digital interfaces |
| **Key Characteristics** | High legibility at small sizes; extensive weight range; optimized for screen rendering |

### 5.5 Typography Rules

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Minimum digital body text size** | 14px — never below 12px for any readable text |
| **Maximum line length (body)** | 70 characters (approx. 640px column width) |
| **Paragraph spacing** | 1.5x the line-height value |
| **Heading-to-body contrast** | Minimum 2-step weight differentiation |
| **Uppercase usage** | Restricted to Overline labels and navigation caps — never for body text |
| **Justified text** | Prohibited — left-aligned only for all body and UI text |
| **Brand Tagline** | Always italic when set in Playfair Display; regular when set in Inter |

### 5.6 Prohibited Typography

- Using system fonts (Arial, Times New Roman, Helvetica) in designed materials
- Setting body copy below 12px in any digital context
- Using more than 2 typefaces in a single layout
- Applying color to body text other than Brand Ink (`#1A1A1A`) or Brand Ivory (`#F7F5F1`)
- Condensing or expanding letterforms manually
- Setting headings in all-lowercase unless expressly directed by campaign creative
- Using italic weight for UI labels or data table content

## Section 6 — Imagery & Photography Direction

### 6.1 Visual Style Definition

**Three-word summary:** *Purposeful. Warm. Operational.*

Dinevique imagery sits at the intersection of the professional and the personal — where a well-run kitchen meets a genuinely great dining experience. Photography should feel documentary rather than staged: real light, real kitchens, real teams. The goal is to make operators see themselves in the imagery, not an aspirational version they cannot relate to.

### 6.2 Photography Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Authentic Environments** | Shoot in real working restaurants, not idealized studio sets. Imperfection signals trust. |
| **Team-Centered** | Prioritize people: the server confident at the table, the cook focused on the pass. Technology supports; people lead. |
| **Operational Light** | Use available light where possible — the glow of a pass ticket window, the warmth of a service lamp. Avoid clinical overhead lighting. |
| **Decisive Moment** | Capture action: an order being placed, a ticket being called, a dish being carried. Still life is secondary. |
| **Restrained Composition** | Follow rule of thirds; allow negative space for text overlay. Avoid cluttered frames. |
| **Technology as Tool** | When devices appear in imagery, they should feel like natural extensions of the workflow — not hero products in a white-room shoot. |

### 6.3 Photography Categories

#### Category 1 — Team & Service Photography

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Style | Documentary, editorial |
| Subjects | Front-of-house staff, waitstaff with handhelds, cashier at terminal |
| Lighting | Warm ambient, overhead service lighting, natural window light |
| Tone | Confident, purposeful, human |
| Avoid | Posed smiles, forced eye contact with camera, sterile studio setups |

#### Category 2 — Kitchen & Operations Photography

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Style | High-contrast, motion-forward |
| Subjects | Kitchen tickets on display, cooks coordinating at the pass, ticket bump moments |
| Lighting | High-contrast with warm tones; permit shadows as compositional elements |
| Tone | Intense, precise, alive |
| Avoid | Glamour food photography, empty kitchens, overly clean staging |

#### Category 3 — Technology & Interface Photography

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Style | Context-in-use; the interface within the environment |
| Subjects | POS terminal in use at counter, KDB mounted in kitchen, handheld in waitstaff's hand |
| Lighting | Screen glow as primary light source acceptable; supplement with warm ambient |
| Tone | Functional, capable, integrated |
| Avoid | Floating device mockups, isolated product shots, white-background device photography |

### 6.4 Image Selection & Usage Rules

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Color treatment** | Images may be subtly warm-toned (+5–10 deg hue shift) to align with brand palette; no filter presets that alter reality |
| **Overlay text** | Ensure minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio between text and image background; apply semi-transparent overlay (rgba(13,13,13,0.55)) where needed |
| **Stock photography** | Permitted for digital editorial use only; must be indistinguishable from brand photography in tone and composition |
| **Image cropping** | Crop to emphasize human subjects; maintain aspect ratios: 16:9 (web), 1:1 (social), 4:5 (social portrait) |
| **Minimum resolution** | 2000px on shortest dimension for web; 300dpi for print at intended size |

## Section 7 — Iconography & Graphic Elements

### 7.1 Icon Style Definition

Dinevique icons follow a **lined, rounded-corner style** with consistent 2px stroke weight at base size (24x24px). Icons are designed for operational contexts — they must be instantly recognizable at small sizes in ambient-lit kitchen and counter environments. The rounded-corner approach humanizes the precision of the system; icons feel approachable without sacrificing clarity. The preferred icon library is **Lucide** (or Phosphor Icons as an alternative), selected for their consistent visual language and Open Source license.

### 7.2 Icon Specifications

| Property | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Style** | Outlined / stroked |
| **Stroke weight** | 2px at 24x24px base; scale proportionally |
| **Corner radius** | 2–3px (medium rounded) |
| **Standard sizes** | 16px (dense UI), 20px (standard UI), 24px (primary), 32px (feature call-out), 48px (section header) |
| **Padding** | 2px internal padding within bounding box at standard size |
| **Color — Default** | Brand Ink (`#1A1A1A`) on light; Brand Ivory (`#F7F5F1`) on dark |
| **Color — Active** | Brand Amber (`#D4820A`) |
| **Color — Disabled** | Muted Mid (`#6B7280`) at 50% opacity |

### 7.3 Icon Usage Rules

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Pairing with text** | Icons used alongside labels; standalone icons require tooltip or ARIA label |
| **Module identification** | Each Platform Module has one signature icon — used consistently across UI and marketing |
| **Navigation icons** | Standard size 24px; active state uses Amber fill (not just stroke) |
| **Prohibited customization** | Do not mix icon styles (e.g., filled and outlined in same UI) |
| **Kitchen Display icons** | Minimum 32px; high-contrast white on dark background only |

### 7.4 Graphic Elements & Signature Patterns

**Connector Line System**
A signature decorative element reflecting the brand's connectivity theme — fine-weight (0.5–1px) horizontal or diagonal lines, drawn in Brand Linen (`#E8E4DC`) on light backgrounds or Brand Slate (`#1E2A38`) on dark. Used as dividers, section headers, and background texture. Never dense enough to create visual noise.

**Module Color Chips**
Compact 4x16px colored chips assigned per Platform Module, used in tables, labels, and navigation to create visual differentiation within the single-brand system.

**Amber Accent Bar**
A 2–4px vertical bar in Brand Amber used at the left edge of highlighted content blocks (callouts, pull quotes, key stats). Appears maximum once per visual section.

### 7.5 Prohibited Iconography

- Icons with sharp 90-degree corners (incompatible with brand's rounded style)
- Emoji used as functional icons in UI or formal documents
- Mixing Lucide/Phosphor icons with Material Design or Font Awesome icons in the same context
- Using icons at sizes below 16px without explicit KDB/POS display approval
- Filled icon style in navigation contexts (reserve filled state for active-only)

## Section 8 — Motion & Animation Principles

### 8.1 Motion Philosophy

Motion in Dinevique is purposeful, not decorative. The platform operates in environments where attention is a premium resource — a cook calling a ticket, a server entering an order under time pressure. Animations must communicate state change, signal completion, and guide focus without demanding it. All motion follows a single guiding principle: **clarity at speed**. Transitions are fast enough to feel responsive; easing is gentle enough to feel premium. No animation should ever make the interface feel slower than it is.

*(Derived from brand's Ruler archetype, operational context, and 70% Innovative / 70% Serious personality spectrum)*

### 8.2 Motion Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Purposeful** | Every animation communicates a state change or guides attention. No idle decoration. |
| **Responsive** | UI responds within 100ms of interaction. Animations never delay actionable feedback. |
| **Restrained** | The default is subtle. Amplitude is earned, not assumed. |
| **Consistent** | Same interaction type uses same easing curve throughout the system. |
| **Accessible** | All animations respect prefers-reduced-motion media query — static equivalents exist for every animated state. |

### 8.3 Easing Standards

| Context | Easing Function | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Page / Screen transitions** | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | 250–300ms |
| **Modal / Drawer open** | cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1) (ease out) | 200ms |
| **Modal / Drawer close** | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1) (ease in) | 150ms |
| **Button / micro-interaction** | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) | 100–120ms |
| **Loading / skeleton pulse** | ease-in-out | 1200ms (loop) |
| **Toast / notification in** | cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1) | 180ms |
| **Toast / notification out** | cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1) | 120ms |

### 8.4 Motion Categories

| Category | Elements | Specification |
|---|---|---|
| **Scroll Reveal** | Section content, feature cards, statistics | Fade up (translateY: 20px to 0, opacity: 0 to 1) at 200ms ease-out; trigger at 20% viewport entry |
| **Hover States** | Buttons, cards, navigation items | Scale 1.0 to 1.02 (cards); background color transition at 100ms |
| **Loading States** | API calls, ticket sync, KDB updates | Skeleton shimmer (left-to-right sweep in Brand Linen); spinner in Brand Amber at 600ms rotation |
| **Ticket State Transitions** | KDB ticket bump, order status change | Flash of Brand Amber (100ms) then settle to new state color; subtle scale pulse |
| **Logo Animation** | Brand intro, loading screen | Wordmark fades in (opacity 0 to 1, 400ms ease-out) after icon mark draws in from center (300ms) |
| **Navigation Active State** | Menu item selection | Left accent bar slides in (width: 0 to 4px, 150ms ease-out) + color transition |

## Section 9 — Voice, Tone & Messaging

### 9.1 Brand Voice Definition

Dinevique speaks like an experienced operations manager who has worked every station in the restaurant — one who understands the weight of a Saturday dinner service, the frustration of a dropped connection, and the satisfaction of a shift that runs without drama. The voice is measured and confident, never excitable. It states capabilities as facts, not promises. It uses the language of restaurant operations because that is the language of the people it serves. It does not over-explain, and it never treats operators as if they need to be impressed — only informed.

### 9.2 Voice Dimensions

| Dimension | Where We Land | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarity** | Concrete over abstract | Operators make decisions under time pressure. Vague claims waste their time. |
| **Authority** | Confident without arrogance | The platform has genuine technical depth; the brand communicates this directly, not boastfully. |
| **Warmth** | Present but measured | Restaurant people are warm; Dinevique reflects that. Warmth is expressed through helpfulness, not exclamation marks. |
| **Precision** | Specific over approximate | "Continues essential workflows during WAN outages" not "works offline". Specificity signals expertise. |
| **Respect** | Peer, not vendor | We speak to operators as capable professionals. |

### 9.3 Voice & Tone by Channel

| Channel | Voice | Tone Guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| **Website / Marketing** | Authoritative, forward-looking | Lead with clarity; end with confidence. No superlatives. Show, do not sell. |
| **App Interface (POS/KDB/Handheld)** | Precise, action-oriented | Minimal words, maximum legibility. Imperative verbs. Never passive. Example: "Print Ticket" not "Ticket Can Be Printed". |
| **Email Communications** | Professional, direct | Short subject lines. No filler openings. Lead with the point. |
| **Social Media** | Informed, slightly more accessible | Slightly warmer register. Still no slang; no excessive punctuation. |
| **Error Messages** | Honest, solution-oriented | State what happened, why, and what to do next. Never blame the user. |
| **Documentation / Support** | Clear, step-by-step | Plain language. Numbered steps. Screenshots preferred over descriptions alone. |
| **Sales / Proposals** | Consultative, evidence-led | Use operator use cases and technical specifics. No generic feature lists. |

### 9.4 Key Messaging Framework

#### Primary Messages

1. **One connected platform.** — Orders, kitchen, handheld, online, and cloud management — all coordinated through Dinevique.
2. **Engineered for continuity.** — Essential operations continue during internet outages; the system catches up when connectivity returns.
3. **Built for how restaurants actually work.** — From a single independent café to a multi-terminal restaurant group, Dinevique is designed for the variability of real hospitality environments.

#### Audience-Specific Value Propositions

| Audience Segment | Primary Pain Point | Dinevique Value Proposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent Restaurant Operators** | Tool fragmentation; too many systems | One platform replaces multiple tools — POS, kitchen display, and online ordering in a single connected system. |
| **Fast-Casual & QSR Groups** | Speed and throughput; reliability during peak | Handheld ordering and kitchen coordination keeps queues moving; local-first architecture means no service interruption during busy periods. |
| **Full-Service Dining** | Table coordination, order accuracy | Tableside handheld ordering reduces order errors; real-time KDB coordination keeps the kitchen in sync with the floor. |
| **Multi-Location Restaurant Groups** | Visibility and consistency across locations | Cloud administration provides centralized menu management, pricing, and reporting across all locations. |
| **Technology-Evaluating Operators** | Reliability and hardware integration concerns | Native Android architecture with direct USB/Serial hardware support; offline-first design with store-and-forward sync. |

### 9.5 Words to Use & Words to Avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Connected | Integrated (overused, imprecise) |
| Operational resilience | Bulletproof, failsafe |
| Engineered | Just "built" in technical contexts |
| Coordinated | Synced (too casual) |
| Clarity | Simplicity (undersells complexity managed) |
| WAN outage / internet interruption | Going offline, offline mode |
| Store-and-forward | Offline sync |
| Platform | App, tool, software (undersells scope) |
| Operators | Users, clients |
| Team | Staff (feels transactional) |
| Hospitality | F&B, foodservice (too jargon-heavy) |

### 9.6 Writing Style Guidelines

| Rule | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Sentence length** | Maximum 25 words for body copy; shorter preferred in UI and social |
| **Passive voice** | Avoid entirely in UI text; minimize in marketing |
| **Capitalization** | Sentence case for body; Title Case for headings and module names only |
| **Numbers** | Numerals for all quantities (e.g., "1 platform", not "one platform"); spell out at sentence start |
| **Abbreviations** | Spell out on first use: Point of Sale (POS), Kitchen Display Board (KDB), Wide Area Network (WAN) |
| **Punctuation** | Oxford comma always; exclamation marks prohibited in all brand contexts |
| **Product references** | Always "Dinevique" (capital D) — never "dinevique" or "DINEVIQUE" in running text |

## Section 15 — Quality Assurance & Governance

### 15.1 Dinevique Brand Consistency Check

| Check | Question | Pass Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Platform Unity** | Does this material reflect the single-brand architecture — Dinevique first, module second? | All modules carry Dinevique prefix; no standalone sub-brand identity visible |
| **2. Color Fidelity** | Are exact hex values used — not approximations or ad-hoc colors? | All colors match the approved palette; no invented colors |
| **3. Operational Tone** | Does the copy speak to operators as peers, using operational language? | No generic tech marketing language; no superlatives; no passive voice in CTAs |
| **4. Accessibility** | Do all text-on-background combinations meet WCAG AA minimum? | Verified against Section 4.6 WCAG table; no Brand Amber used for small body text on light backgrounds |
| **5. Resilience Narrative** | Does the material, where relevant, communicate the offline-resilience differentiator? | The offline-continuity capability is not omitted from feature discussions |

### 15.2 Quality Protocol

**Layer 1 — Creator Self-Check**
The creator applies the 5-point brand consistency check and verifies visual specifications from Sections 3–8 before submission.

**Layer 2 — Brand Owner Review**
The designated Brand Owner reviews for strategic consistency, positioning alignment, and governance compliance. Feedback returned within 2 business days.

**Layer 3 — External Material Compliance**
Any material produced by an external vendor, agency, or partner undergoes both Layer 1 and Layer 2 review before publication.

### 15.3 Brand Audit Schedule

| Frequency | Scope | Responsible |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Website, social media, app UI screenshots — check for color/typography drift | Brand Owner |
| **Quarterly** | Full document audit — proposals, pitch decks, email templates | Brand Owner + Product Lead |
| **Bi-Annually** | Full brand guideline review — update if platform, audience, or market positioning has changed | Brand Owner + Leadership |
| **On Launch** | Every new module, feature, or campaign — full Section 15.1 check before go-live | Creator + Brand Owner |

### 15.4 Approval Process

| Material Type | Approval Authority |
|---|---|
| Website copy and design updates | Brand Owner |
| Social media posts (standard) | Brand Owner or designated Social Manager |
| Campaign creative (paid) | Brand Owner + Leadership |
| External partnership materials | Brand Owner + Legal |
| Press / media mentions of the brand | Brand Owner + Leadership |
| Internal documentation | Team Lead (no formal approval required) |

### 15.5 Brand Deviation Protocol

1. **Document** — Record the proposed deviation: what is different, why it is required, and scope of use.
2. **Escalate** — Bring the documented deviation to the Brand Owner within 24 hours before production.
3. **Limit** — If approved, the deviation is limited to the specific material/context specified. It does not set a precedent.
4. **Revert** — If emergency-approved for time-sensitive use, the standard version replaces it at the next update cycle.
5. **Log** — All approved deviations are logged in the Brand Deviation Register (maintained by the Brand Owner).

### 15.6 New Team Member Onboarding

1. Receive link to this Brand Guidelines document on Day 1
2. Complete a 30-minute brand orientation with the Brand Owner (or async video equivalent)
3. Read Sections 1, 4, 5, and 9 in full before producing any materials
4. First 3 materials produced by new team members are reviewed by the Brand Owner before publication
5. Sign off on the brand guidelines acknowledgement form (TBD) within first 2 weeks

## Section 16 — Appendices

### 16.1 Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **WAN** | Wide Area Network — the internet connection between the restaurant premise and cloud services. Dinevique is engineered to continue essential operations during WAN outages. |
| **LAN** | Local Area Network — the in-premises Wi-Fi and wired network over which the Master Terminal communicates with KDB and Handheld devices. |
| **KDB** | Kitchen Display Board — the Dinevique Kitchen module; a native Android display mounted in the kitchen for ticket management. |
| **Master Terminal** | The primary Dinevique POS tablet at the front-of-house counter; acts as the local orchestrator for all in-premises device communication. |
| **Store-and-Forward** | The mechanism by which the Master Terminal queues outbound events during a WAN outage and drains them to the cloud when connectivity is restored. |
| **Idempotent Sync** | A data synchronization approach that ensures duplicated or retried payloads produce no additional side effects — critical for accurate financial and order records during unstable reconnections. |
| **Brand Amber** | Dinevique's primary accent color (`#D4820A`) — used for CTAs, active states, and key highlights. Must not be used for body text on light backgrounds. |
| **Brand Onyx** | Dinevique's primary dark background color (`#0D0D0D`). |
| **Brand Slate** | Dinevique's secondary dark background color (`#1E2A38`) — used for panels, cards, and contrast sections. |
| **Brand Ivory** | Dinevique's light background color (`#F7F5F1`) — warm off-white used for light-mode content areas and print backgrounds. |
| **WCAG** | Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — the international standard for digital accessibility. Dinevique targets WCAG AA compliance (4.5:1 contrast ratio) as minimum for all text-on-background combinations. |
| **Platform Module** | One of the five functional areas of the Dinevique platform: POS, Kitchen, Handheld, Online, Cloud. All modules operate under the master Dinevique brand. |
| **Brand Deviation** | A documented, Brand Owner-approved exception to any standard defined in this document. Deviations are limited, logged, and time-bound. |
| **Tagline** | *The Art of Smarter Dining.* — Dinevique's brand tagline. Always with a full stop. Never modified or paraphrased. |
| **Single-Brand Architecture** | Dinevique's brand model in which all platform modules operate under one master brand identity. No module has an independent brand or logo. |
